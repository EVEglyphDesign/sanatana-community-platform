The community digital twin

A digital twin is a record of a thing, kept by the thing itself, structured well enough that software can read it, and held nowhere the subject cannot reach.

What this twin records

LayerContents
InstitutionAddress, hours, governance documents, published observances, contact routes, class roster.
CalendarFestivals and recurring pujas as dates, so they can be read by a phone rather than a poster.
EducationThe ladder, the units covered, the terms completed. Per cohort, not per named child.
ServiceSeva entries in aggregate — acts of help recorded, with names only where the family chose to give them.
ProvenanceWhere every fact came from, when it was checked, and whether the community has confirmed it.

What this twin refuses to record

  • Children's names, photographs, ages or schools without a parent's written consent, held in the repository.
  • Attendance as a compliance instrument. A family that misses a term owes no explanation to a file.
  • Donation amounts against named individuals.
  • Any tracker, advertisement, analytics beacon or third-party script on these pages.
  • Anything a member cannot ask to have removed and see removed in the commit history.
Safety first, betterment second. Where a feature would make the record better but the community less safe, the feature loses. That ordering is not negotiable and it is inherited from the operating canon, not invented here.

Consent, in practice

  1. Nothing about a named person enters this repository until they, or a parent, say so in writing.
  2. Consent names the layer and the reader. "Yes to the class roster, no to photographs" is a valid answer.
  3. Withdrawal is a single request and takes effect in the next commit. No form, no committee.
  4. The consent record lives in the repository beside the data, so the two cannot drift apart.

Why a family would want one

Because the alternative is that the record of a community's life sits inside somebody else's product, priced by seat, exportable only in the shapes that product allows. A twin kept as plain text under version control outlives the platform, the steward and the subscription. In thirty years a grandchild can still read it.
